The Art of Hand-Pressing Wildflowers for Deckle Cards

There is magic in catching a flower at peak bloom. We harvest after dew lifts, press between blotters under sal-wood weights for 3–4 weeks, then lay each petal onto wet cotton pulp so fibres grip it forever.

AS Ananya SharmaOctober 2026 • Jaipur Paper Unit Post Share
There is magic in catching a flower at peak bloom — press it right and it keeps its gold for decades.

Thin Petals Win

Cosmos, bougainvillea, cornflower and marigold keep pigment best. Thick roses brown — so we use them sparingly, as confetti flecks.

We press cosmos, marigold, cornflower and bougainvillea between blotting sheets under sal-wood weights for three to four weeks. Thin petals keep their pigment best; thick roses brown, so they become confetti flecks instead of centrepieces.

Once dry, each petal is laid by tweezers onto wet cotton pulp so the fibres grip it forever. The sheet then goes under the Heidelberg for its letterpress kiss — a card that holds a garden, ready to be written on.

Studio note — every card shown here is printed on 300 GSM recycled cotton rag, letterpressed on our 1950s Heidelberg and finished with gold foil in Jaipur.
